## 1. Scope

hangel ("we", "us", "our") is an APP entity for the purposes of section 6 of the *Privacy Act 1988*. This policy explains how we handle personal information of Australian users in compliance with the 13 Australian Privacy Principles. It also addresses the 2024 amendments introducing a statutory tort for serious invasions of privacy and an OAIC-administered Children's Online Privacy Code (commencing 10 December 2026).

## 2. The 13 Australian Privacy Principles (APPs) — how we apply them

- **APP 1 — Open and transparent management.** We publish this policy and review it annually.
- **APP 2 — Anonymity and pseudonymity.** Where practicable you can interact with limited Service features (e.g. reading public goals) without identifying yourself.
- **APP 3 — Collection of solicited personal information.** We collect only what is reasonably necessary.
- **APP 4 — Unsolicited personal information.** We destroy or de-identify unsolicited information unless it could have been lawfully collected.
- **APP 5 — Notification of collection.** This policy and our in-app just-in-time notices serve as APP 5 notification.
- **APP 6 — Use and disclosure.** We use information only for the primary purpose disclosed or a directly related secondary purpose you would reasonably expect.
- **APP 7 — Direct marketing.** Marketing is opt-in; every message contains a clear unsubscribe consistent with the Spam Act 2003.
- **APP 8 — Cross-border disclosure.** See section 5 below.
- **APP 9 — Government identifiers.** We do not adopt, use or disclose government identifiers (TFN, Medicare number).
- **APP 10 — Quality.** We maintain accuracy via in-app self-service editing.
- **APP 11 — Security.** Encryption in transit and at rest, role-based access, vendor due diligence.
- **APP 12 — Access.** You can request a copy of your personal information; we respond within 30 days.
- **APP 13 — Correction.** You can correct inaccurate information at any time via Settings.

## 3. Personal information we collect

We collect: (a) identity & account data — name, email, phone, date of birth for age verification; (b) profile & content data — photos, posts, goals; (c) device & technical data — IP address, advertising identifier (where permitted), device model, OS version, crash logs; (d) usage data — feature interactions, session duration; (e) location data — approximate or, with separate consent, precise device location; (f) communications data — support tickets, survey responses; (g) payment data — handled by Apple, Google and Stripe; we receive only a transaction reference.

We do not solicit *sensitive information* (health, racial or ethnic origin, political opinions, religious beliefs, sexual orientation, biometrics) within the meaning of section 6 of the Act unless you voluntarily upload it, in which case processing is based on **express consent** that you may withdraw at any time.

## 4. Children

The Service is general-audience. Account creation requires age **13 or above**. From 10 December 2026, the OAIC Children's Online Privacy Code will impose additional duties on services accessed by under-18s; we will publish a separate notice for under-18 users when the Code commences and will adopt age-appropriate default settings (minimum data collection, no targeted advertising, no precise geolocation).

## 6. Notifiable Data Breaches scheme

We have a documented data-breach response plan. Where a breach is likely to result in *serious harm*, we notify the OAIC and affected individuals **as soon as practicable** under Part IIIC of the Privacy Act. Non-compliance with the NDB scheme may attract civil penalties under section 13G of up to **AUD 50 million, three times the benefit obtained from the misuse, or 30% of adjusted turnover** (whichever is greater) — substantially increased by the 2022 amendments.

## 7. Retention

We retain account data for the life of your account plus 24 months for fraud prevention and legal claims. Server access logs are retained for 12 months. You may request earlier deletion by emailing privacy@hangel.org.

## 8. Complaints

You may complain to our Privacy Officer first; if unresolved within 30 days, you may complain to the OAIC. From 2025 onwards, individuals also have a direct statutory cause of action for serious invasions of privacy under the Privacy and Other Legislation Amendment Act 2024.
