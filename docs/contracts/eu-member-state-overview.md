## 1. Purpose

The GDPR (Regulation (EU) 2016/679) is directly applicable but contains over 50 "opening clauses" allowing Member States to legislate further. ePrivacy is a Directive — Member State transposition varies substantially. This document summarises the most operationally-relevant divergences for hangel:

- Lead supervisory authority + portal.
- Digital age of consent (Art. 8).
- Cookie/tracker consent baseline (ePrivacy Art. 5(3) transposition).
- Health data conditions (Art. 9(4)).
- Notable derogations affecting donor / charity activities.
- Mandatory representative / DPO triggers beyond GDPR baseline.

## 3. EU Representative (Art. 27)

hangel, established in Türkiye, must appoint an EU Representative. Pragmatic choice criteria:

- Located in a Member State where a material number of data subjects reside (Recital 80).
- Authorised to interact with all relevant SAs.
- Acts as the single point of contact for SAs and data subjects.

Candidate jurisdictions for the EU Rep based on hangel's projected user base: **Germany, France, Netherlands, Ireland.** Final selection requires legal + commercial evaluation.

## 4. Lead Supervisory Authority

Until hangel designates a Member State main establishment (Art. 4(16)) within the Union, **no lead SA** applies — each SA may act independently for processing affecting subjects in its territory (One-Stop-Shop only available to controllers with EU main establishment).

Strategic options:
- (a) Remain Türkiye-based → multiple parallel SAs.
- (b) Establish an EU subsidiary handling EU operations → single lead SA in that Member State.

## 5. Cookie / Tracker Consent Baseline

The strictest enforcement currently observed:

1. **France (CNIL):** Accept and Reject must be equally easy; refusing cannot be more onerous (CNIL deliberations 2021–2024, €60M+ fines on BigTech).
2. **Germany (TTDSG + BGH Planet49):** No legitimate-interest fallback for cookies; explicit consent required.
3. **Italy (Garante 10.06.2021):** Equal-prominence Accept/Reject; "scroll = consent" banned.
4. **Netherlands (AP):** Cookiewalls only acceptable if equivalent alternative offered.

Operational recommendation: implement a Consent Management Platform (CMP) compliant with IAB TCF v2.2 + CNIL recommendations as the floor; finer adjustments per Member State via geo-targeting.

## 6. Health-Data Conditions (Art. 9(4))

Member States may maintain or introduce further conditions on processing of genetic data, biometric data, or data concerning health. Operationally relevant for hangel (blood-donor connections):

- **France:** HDS certification (Hébergeur de Données de Santé) for hosting providers — Cloud provider must be certified.
- **Italy:** Garante deliberation on processing of health data outside of healthcare provision contexts.
- **Germany:** Land-level health laws + federal Sozialgesetzbuch V.
- **Spain:** LOPDGDD Disposición Adicional 17 on biomedical research data.

Where hangel handles only **blood-group + donation-eligibility metadata** (not full medical records), the regime is lighter but Art. 9(2)(a) explicit consent + Member State conditions still apply.

## 7. Charity / NGO Sector Specifics

Several Member States have NGO-specific transparency or processing rules (HU NAIH, RO ANSPDCP, BG KZLD). hangel partners with local NGOs and brand sponsors — DPA terms must reflect the relevant Member State requirements.

## 9. DPO Designation

Mandatory under Art. 37 where:
- Core activities involve large-scale processing of special categories — **likely triggers for hangel** (blood-group data of users).
- Member States may impose additional triggers (DE BDSG §38: ≥20 employees handling personal data automatically).

Recommendation: appoint a DPO regardless of Member State (good practice + future expansion).

## 10. Data Transfers

All transfers outside EU/EEA must rest on Chapter V mechanism. For Türkiye-bound transfers (where hangel HQ servers reside): **no adequacy decision**. Use:
- SCC 2021/914 Module Two/Three/Four depending on roles, plus
- Transfer Impact Assessment (TIA) addressing Turkish surveillance law (LEPDP, Law No. 5651), and
- Supplementary technical measures (encryption at rest + in transit with keys held in EU, strict access logging).
